Is it ready for fabrication?

The design is complete and passes ChipFoundry's own precheck in ChipFoundry's own tool versions (14 of 14 checks). It cannot be ordered yet: ChipFoundry has not confirmed that any shuttle currently accepts the ReRAM layer. Their ReRAM page still advertises a "November 2025 ChipCreate Shuttle", and their FAQ says "chipIgnite does not initially support ReRam." That question and three more (below) must be answered by ChipFoundry; none of them can be settled from the design files. Say "design complete, passes the foundry's precheck, waiting for the foundry's confirmation of ReRAM", not "ready to fabricate".

What "verified" means here, and what it cannot mean

proven or checked exactly

  • The logic does what it is specified to do. Every product is compared with exact integer arithmetic, on two simulators, in both trit codes, through the chip controller, and on an FPGA board.
  • The tests are able to fail. Every deliberately injected bug was caught (core 75/75 and 81/81, chip controller 26/26, proofs 9/9).
  • What is drawn is what is simulated. LVS: the layout equals the circuit netlist; formal proof: the netlist given to place and route equals the RTL.
  • The layout obeys the foundry's rules. DRC 0, antenna 0 in two checkers, ChipFoundry's precheck 14 of 14.

verified against models

  • The circuits work in simulation with SkyWater's own transistor models, over process corners, −40 to 125 °C, supply ±10 %, transistor mismatch and the drawn wiring.
  • Reading and programming work for devices that behave like the device model, which is fitted to SkyWater's published ReRAM data, and keep working at twice the published spread.
  • A model can be wrong in ways a simulation cannot reveal. That is why the chip has a test cell with direct pad access and programming voltages set from the board.

cannot be known before silicon

  • How the ReRAM devices on this shuttle actually behave: their spread, forming voltage, how many write cycles they survive, how long they hold a state.
  • Therefore what fraction of the 1,728 weights program correctly on the first chips, and how accurate the analog read is.
  • These are what the test chip is for (section 12).

Does it work as intended? The logic: yes, every check against exact arithmetic passes and every planted bug is found. The circuits: yes, in simulation, with the stated margins. The chip as a whole: it will work as intended if the ReRAM devices behave within the range the design was checked for (up to twice SkyWater's published spread). That last condition is a property of the devices, not of the design, and only measurements of a fabricated chip can settle it.

What you can tell your professor

accurate

  • TCIM0 stores a 72 × 24 matrix of ternary weights (−1, 0, +1) in ReRAM and multiplies it with a 72-trit vector, returning 24 integers from −72 to +72; one product replaces 72 TMAC.T instructions of REBEL-6.
  • Each weight is two ReRAM devices, each read as one bit (2T2R), so the digital result is exact whenever every device is read correctly.
  • A complete test chip for SkyWater SKY130 (sky130B, ReRAM option) in ChipFoundry's Caravan analog harness has been designed and drawn: 3,456 ReRAM devices, all drivers, 48 sense amplifiers, the controller, the logic, pads, and a separate test cell.
  • The layout passes design-rule checks, LVS and the antenna rule at every level, and ChipFoundry's own precheck (14/14) in ChipFoundry's pinned tool and PDK versions.
  • The logic is verified by simulation in two simulators, fault injection and formal proofs, also after layout.
  • Device behaviour rests on SkyWater's open model and on a fit to SkyWater's published yields; the device spread is uncertain by a factor of about seven, which is what the chip is meant to measure.
  • Programming levels come from the test board, so they can be changed after the first measurement.

do not claim

  • "Ready to fabricate" or "taped out": ReRAM availability is unconfirmed and nothing has been submitted.
  • "It will work on silicon": the devices are modelled, not measured.
  • That ChipFoundry offers ReRAM on the next shuttle, or that it costs $14,950: neither is published for ReRAM.
  • That the pads are ESD-protected: the analog pads used are not.
  • That the chip does analog (multi-row) products: the SKY130 chip has no converter; digital mode only.
  • That an IHP chip is ready: IHP's ReRAM kit has not been available; only the parts the open PDK allows are designed.
  • Energy or speed advantages: the energy figures are estimates, and at this size the case for ReRAM is non-volatile storage density, not energy.

1 · The idea

REBEL-6 is the balanced-ternary processor of the research group at the University of South-Eastern Norway. A neural-network layer with ternary weights is a matrix-vector product, which REBEL-6 computes one multiply-accumulate (TMAC.T) at a time. TCIM0 is a memory-mapped device that holds the matrix in resistive memory and computes the whole product, 72 rows by 24 columns, in one command.

For column j the device returns Y[j] = Σ W[r][j] · X[r] over the 72 rows, with weights and inputs in {−1, 0, +1}. The size follows REBEL-6's word of 24 trits: 72 rows are three input words, one row of weights is one word, and each result fits a 6-trit tryte.

2 · One weight is two resistors (2T2R)

A ReRAM device is a resistor with two stable states. A set pulse grows a conducting filament (low resistance, LRS); a reset pulse of opposite polarity breaks it (high resistance, HRS). Each weight uses two devices, each with its own select transistor, on two bit lines BL+ and BL−. Because each device is either on or off, it is read as one bit, and the weight is recovered exactly.

Why two levels and not four? The original draft stored more than one bit per device. Two clearly separated states are far easier to program and read, and they make the digital result exact rather than approximate.

The device model. The Stanford-PKU compact model (the equations of SkyWater's open model): the current falls exponentially with the gap between filament tip and electrode, and the tip moves exponentially faster with voltage and temperature, which is why switching has a sharp threshold. It is implemented three times (Python, ngspice, Verilog-A) and the implementations agree. It has no forming, no retention loss and no cycle-to-cycle randomness: it is a stand-in for the foundry's own measurements.

The fit to SkyWater's silicon. SkyWater publishes one yield statistic: with one 1 µs pulse, 90.83 % of cells reset and 97.45 % set. Fitting the model's reset rate and device-to-device spread to those two numbers (testing three transistor widths and three pass criteria, because SkyWater does not publish them) leaves the spread between 0.53 and 3.48 times the assumed baseline: a factor of about seven that only measurement can remove. It also shows that SkyWater's published model parameters reset far too slowly: the reset rate has to be raised by 2.3 to 8.9 orders of magnitude.

3 · From the chip down to the atoms: the layers and what each one does

A chip is built as a stack of layers on a silicon wafer: transistors in the silicon at the bottom, then alternating layers of metal wiring and insulating glass above. SkyWater's sky130B process adds the ReRAM device inside this stack, between the first and the second metal layer. Every picture in this section is drawn from the design's own layout file (GDS) or from the process data, not by hand; where something is not published, the picture says so.

Inside the ReRAM: nanometres

The same device as an object you can operate: cut in half through its filament, with the gap under your pointer. The numbers are the model the chip was designed with.

How the switching works (SkyWater's description). An electric field across the oxide creates and moves oxygen vacancies, places where an oxygen atom is missing from the HfO2. Where enough vacancies line up they form a conducting filament from one electrode toward the other; the remaining gap sets the resistance, because current must tunnel across it, and the current falls exponentially as the gap grows. The thin Ti layer is commonly described in this kind of cell as an oxygen-exchange layer that takes up oxygen and so supplies vacancies; SkyWater's documentation shows the layer but does not describe its role.

  1. Forming, once. A fresh device conducts below 0.1 µS. A first, higher pulse (SkyWater: 2.2 to 3.1 V, 1 µs) creates the filament and leaves the device at 30–120 µS. On this chip the test board does it through the ordinary programming path with a higher word-line rail. The model has no fresh state, so forming is not simulated.
  2. Set (to the low-resistance state). The bit line (top electrode, metal2) goes positive, the source line stays at ground; the word-line voltage limits the current and so where the device lands. Proposed map: word line 2.0 V, bit line VPRG = 1.4 V + 0.1 V per pulse step, until the device reads above the set target (24 µS nominal).
  3. Reset (to the high-resistance state). The opposite polarity: the source line goes up to VPRG and the bit line is grounded, which shortens the filament and widens the gap. Proposed map: for steps 0–5 the word line rises from 2.5 V by 0.1 V per step with VPRG at 2.4 V; from step 6 the word line stays at 3.0 V and VPRG rises from 2.5 V by 0.1 V per step, until the device reads below the reset target (8 µS nominal).
  4. Read. The bit line is held at 0.2 V and the source line at ground, far below the programming levels. The sense amplifier compares the current with the reference (section 4). How long a state survives many reads (read disturb) and time (retention) on SkyWater's devices is not in the model; the chip is meant to measure it.

4 · Reading: the sense amplifier and its three references

The reference has three switchable levels made of 2, 3 and 6 units of about 0.8 µA at 0.2 V, i.e. 8, 12 and 24 µS nominal: the reset target (a device must read below it after a reset), the read reference between the two states, and the set target (a device must read above it after a set). The gaps between them are guard bands: a device that passes its verify is far from the read reference.

5 · Programming: pulse-and-verify

The select transistor's gate voltage (the word line) limits the current through the device and so sets where a set lands. On this chip the amplitudes are not generated on chip: the chip tells the test board which pulse comes next (pulse kind pk, amplitude code pamp, strobe pstb), and the board sets the rails. That keeps the scheme changeable after the first measurement.

The programming was simulated through the drawn drivers, cell by cell, with SkyWater's device parameters and the fitted reset rate. A 1 µs pulse with this model is too stiff for the transient solver, so each pulse is integrated quasi-statically (the circuit's DC solution at each gap, the gap integrated with the model's rate equation); where the transient does converge, the two agree within about 0.4 %.

SkyWater's own set condition (1.7 V on the word line) does not reach the target at the slow corner: the 1 µm select transistor limits the current. At 2.0 V every corner reaches it, in 7–8 pulses with nominal wiring and 9–11 with the worst wiring. Reset reaches its target within 1–2 pulses with the fitted rate, and within 8 pulses for cells up to three decades slower; cells four decades slower fail at the slow corner and −40 °C. No unselected device changed in any case. The proposed board map is therefore: set at 2.0 V on the word line, VPRG 1.4 V + 0.1 V per code; reset stepped from 2.5 V. Forming is done by the board as ordinary program commands with a higher word-line rail; the model has no pristine state, so forming is not simulated.

6 · The test chip

SkyWater's ReRAM layer, rules and model are public, so a complete chip with real ReRAM can be drawn and checked with open tools. The chip is drawn for ChipFoundry's analog harness, Caravan: a user area of 2.92 × 3.52 mm with 27 general-purpose pads, 11 direct analog pads, a 1.8 V and a 3.3 V supply. Every ReRAM operating level is at most 3.3 V, so all array-side circuits use SKY130's 5 V transistors on the 3.3 V supply; logic and sensing use the 1.8 V core transistors.

Protection. Caravan's direct analog pads have no ESD diodes of their own; the harness offers clamps on io_analog[4–6] that this design does not connect. 7 · The array controller and break-before-make

In the macro each bit line goes to the pulse rail VPRG when its prg control is high, to its sense amplifier when the shared bl_sen is high, and to ground when none of prg, bl_sen, flt is high. The ground switch is a NOR gate in the 3.3 V domain next to each driver, so a line is grounded by default and the controller needs one wire per bit line plus four shared ones.

One request runs IDLE → BRK → MAKE → SETL → WLON → WLOFF → REL → BRK2 → GND → IDLE. Every output to the macro comes straight from a flip-flop, so no logic glitch reaches the level shifters. Times are set by pins: T_SETTLE = 2ts+1, T_PULSE = 2tp+2, T_SENSE = 2tr+2 clock cycles; with a 16 MHz board clock, tp = 2 gives 16 cycles = 1 µs.

In a reset pulse the column's source line is at VPRG; a device in that column that must not change keeps its bit line at VPRG too, so it sees no voltage (inhibit). The simulation of a full set / reset / read sequence through the drawn slice shows break-before-make lowers the peak current drawn from the rail during switching from 1,004 to 685 µA in a reset.

How it is tested. The full TCIM0 test bench runs through the controller with a line-level model of the macro that checks every clock cycle: no rail shorted, no sense input driven, at most one word line, no line floating under a word line or for more than two cycles, exact pulse and sense lengths. 76,823 / 76,973 / 79,228 checks pass in three timing settings; 26 of 26 injected controller faults are detected. One fault survived at first (a reset verify against the wrong reference); a directed test was added and it is now caught.

8 · The logic design and its verification

The logic is written in SystemVerilog and is the same in simulation, on the FPGA and on the chip. A host talks to it through a four-wire serial port; behind the port is a register block with three sequencers that do the work.

The serial frame

What a product does, step by step

Programming a row (PCMD = +): the sequencer compares PDATA with what each device must become, then runs pulse-and-verify (section 5) in two phases, reset first and set second, pulsing only the devices that still fail their verify, at amplitude steps 0 to 20. If a device has not passed after the last step, WERR is set. PITER reports how many pulses the row took. Reading a row back (PCMD = −) fills PDATA from the array, so the host can check every weight.

How the logic was checked

Why fault injection. A passing test proves little alone. Copies of the logic with one deliberate fault each must all be caught. The revision 3 test bench passed 60,401 checks yet missed 30 of 66 faults and two real bugs; both were fixed and the tests extended until every fault is detected. Every LVS, DRC, proof and gate-level run was also run once with a planted error and had to report it.

The logic block on the chip is placed and routed with OpenROAD-flow-scripts on sky130B: 9,638 logic cells (1,074 flip-flops, 56 clock gates) plus 610 antenna diodes, 550 × 343 µm, 47.9 % utilization.

The gate-level run uses Verilator; CXXRTL, used for the earlier gate-level runs, did not run this flattened netlist correctly and the cause was not found. The Verilator run fails as it must with one gate changed and with a clock gate stuck.

9 · Proof on hardware: TCIM0 on an FPGA board

Before any chip exists, the logic was run on a real board: a Sipeed Tang Nano 9K with a Gowin GW1NR-9 FPGA, on 10 October 2026. The array is emulated inside the FPGA, so this proves the logic, the serial protocol and the programming loop at speed on hardware; it says nothing about ReRAM devices themselves.

What one run does. Program a random 72 × 24 ternary matrix (one third zeros) row by row with pulse-and-verify; read every row back and count wrong weights; run 100 random digital products and 100 analog products and compare every column with exact arithmetic. Three emulated arrays were used: the published device spread (× 1) and two and three times that, to see how the logic behaves when devices misbehave.

What it shows. With the published spread every weight is programmed correctly and every digital product is exact, on the board as in simulation. With two and three times the spread some devices cannot be programmed within the 21 steps; the sequencer raises WERR on those rows instead of computing silently with wrong weights, which is the designed behaviour. The analog mode (not on the SKY130 chip) is off by 1 to 2 in most products even at the published spread, as expected from device spread without calibration. The gate-level simulation of the synthesized FPGA netlist gave numbers identical to the RTL simulation.

What the board caught. The first bitstream computed every negative analog sum wrongly (364 at worst, the converter's full scale) while every simulation passed: the emulator used the FPGA's hard multiplier with signed operands, and the simulation model of that cell, written from its documentation, did not behave like the silicon. The emulator now uses unsigned products only, and the build fails if a signed multiplier appears. TCIM0 itself was not affected. The lesson is kept for the chip: a gate-level run is only as good as its cell models.

10 · Layout and sign-off

All layout is generated by script with Magic. Twelve periphery cells, the analog macro (1,436.7 × 1,391.7 µm) and the full user area each pass DRC with the full rules and match their netlists; at each level a planted error was caught. The array is 86.4 × 216 µm: 3.6 × 3.0 µm per 2T2R cell, the ReRAM element between metal1 and metal2, the select transistor 1.0 / 0.5 µm.

The antenna rule, two checkers

At the top level, 61 + 3 diodes protect the inputs on long tracks. Inside the logic block, two tools with the same SkyWater limits disagreed: OpenROAD reported 0 violations, Magic flagged 4 gates. On one gate they agreed within 5 % (both near the limit); on two others Magic's figure for the via between metal4 and metal5 is about four times stricter than the rule file OpenROAD reads, and why was not found. Rather than choose, the block was re-routed with a second antenna repair at a 76 % margin: 610 diodes, logic netlist unchanged line for line, and 0 violations in both checkers (OpenROAD 0, Magic 0).

11 · ChipFoundry's precheck in ChipFoundry's own versions

ChipFoundry's precheck pins its tools and PDK: Magic 8.3.471, netgen 1.5.272, CVC v1.1.7, KLayout 0.29.12 and open_pdks 3e0e31d (September 2025). The first 14-of-14 result used newer versions (Magic 8.3.685, open_pdks 1e35547 of October 2026). Running the precheck in their exact versions on 12 October found two differences between the PDK versions, one at a time:

  1. Batch 19, LVS. Their LVS reported a property error in the tie cell conb_1 (35 in the logic block): the two versions mark its polysilicon resistors with different lengths.
  2. Batch 21, with every standard cell taken from their PDK: LVS passed, but their KLayout FEOL check found one npc.2 violation at an and2_0 next to an a21boi_0: the older and2_0 has a notch in its nitride-poly-cut (NPC) shape that leaves a 0.16 µm gap where 0.27 µm is required. The newer PDK fills the notch.

Comparing the two libraries shape by shape, only four cells differ in geometry: and2_0 (NPC), and4b_2 (one poly shape), conb_1 (resistor marker and NPC) and o211a_2 (not used). The netlists differ only in conb_1. The chip therefore takes conb_1 from ChipFoundry's PDK and every other cell from the newer one (layout/sky130/periph/stdcells_cf), and was checked again: LVS match without property errors, DRC 0, antenna 0 in both checkers, and ChipFoundry's precheck in their versions (batch 22):

12 · Limits: what cannot be known before silicon

KindCan it be made certain before silicon?State
LogicYesdone: exact reference, two simulators, fault injection, formal proofs, checks after layout
Circuit marginsYes, against a stated range of device behaviourdone: corners, mismatch, temperature, drawn wiring
Physical correctnessYesdone: DRC, LVS, antenna, the foundry's precheck in its own versions
Device behaviourNoassumed from published data; spread uncertain by a factor of about seven

The design answers the last row with structure: a two-state read that tolerates twice the published spread, a digital mode independent of analog accuracy, programming that stops on a verify read, programming levels from the board, and a test cell with direct pad access.

Known simplifications and soft points (stated so they are not mistaken for results):

  • The device model has no forming, retention loss, read-current temperature dependence or cycle-to-cycle randomness; forming is not simulated.
  • Programming is simulated quasi-statically, checked against transient simulation where both converge.
  • Logic timing on sky130B uses the platform's sky130A extraction rules, with ±20 % parasitics as a stand-in for corners.
  • Analog cells were simulated from their schematics (LVS proves the layout matches); the long wires of the macro were assessed separately; the cells' short internal wiring was not back-annotated.
  • The set target's spread under mismatch may be up to about 1.6 times the simulated one; covered by the worst-case programming run and the read margin.
  • The two antenna checkers disagree on one via figure by about four times; the block was re-routed until both pass.
  • Pads 0–4 are unused; the precheck's circuit checker (CVC) notes three of their user-side controls as "unknown voltage", a soft warning that passes. ChipFoundry's own example leaves them undriven the same way.
  • No ESD protection on the analog pads used.

Corrections made today (12 October)

  • Pulse length example. The report said T_PULSE = 1 µs at tp = 4; at 16 MHz that is 4 µs. Correct: tp = 2 gives 16 cycles = 1 µs.
  • Read margin. The margin of the lowest set target used only one library's read-reference maximum. Correct: 21.55 µS is 7.1 µS above the highest read reference of either library (14.46 µS).
  • Standard cells. The chip used the cells of open_pdks 1e35547; ChipFoundry checks with 3e0e31d. Their LVS reported conb_1, and with all their cells their FEOL check reported one and2_0. The chip now takes conb_1 from their PDK and the rest from the newer one, and passes their precheck in their versions (section 11).
  • PC copy. 70 files were missing from the PC copy; they were copied and every file checked by md5.
